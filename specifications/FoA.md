## Vision

To build a world where compassion for animals is a universal human value, inspiring people to treat all animals with respect, kindness, and care.

## Mission

To nurture compassion for animals as a lifelong human value, especially among children and young adults, through awareness, education, creative engagement, community participation, and positive action.

## Objectives for the Next Six Months

1. **Build and launch the digital platform.** Develop the first functional version of the platform through which young participants can register, participate in competitions, submit their work, receive recognition and remain connected with the organization.
2. **Launch pilot competitions.** Conduct initial drawing, painting, art, craft, essay, photography and videography competitions centred around animals, compassion and animal well-being.
3. **Engage schools and colleges.** Establish partnerships with selected educational institutions to reach children and young adults and encourage participation in the initiative.
4. **Build the first community.** Convert participants from one-time competition entrants into members of an ongoing community interested in animals and animal well-being.
5. **Develop an animal-welfare network.** Begin building a structured database of volunteers, animal-welfare organizations and other relevant stakeholders that can eventually form part of the global platform.
6. **Build awareness through social media.** Establish a consistent social-media presence that uses educational, creative and participant-generated content to increase awareness of animal welfare and encourage compassionate attitudes towards animals.
7. **Develop the volunteer network.** Engage students and other young volunteers in technology, content creation, outreach, competition management, social media and community-building activities.
8. **Measure and learn.** Track participation, engagement, institutional partnerships, volunteer involvement, social-media reach and participant feedback to understand what works before expanding the initiative.

## Friends of Animals (FOA) — Stakeholder Framework

### 1. Education & Institutional Stakeholders

These stakeholders enable FOA to reach and engage young people.

- Schools / Colleges and Universities
- Individual Participants
- Teachers / School-College Coordinators
- Parents and Guardians (in case of minors)

### 3. FOA Organizational & Operational Stakeholders

These stakeholders operate and govern the organization and its platform.

- FOA Administrators
- Program and Competition Coordinators
- Technology / Engineering Team
- Social Media and Content Team
- Community Managers
- Finance / Accounts Team
- Volunteers

### 4. Creative Competitions & Engagement Stakeholders

These stakeholders participate in or support FOA's creative engagement programs.

- Competition Participants (institution and individual)
- Judges / Jury Members

These stakeholders support activities such as drawing, painting, art, craft, essay writing, photography and videography.

### 5. Animal-Welfare Ecosystem

These stakeholders bring animal-welfare knowledge, credibility and opportunities for action into the FOA community.

- Animal-Welfare NGOs (will register here for database)
- Animal Rescue Organizations
- Independent Animal Rescuers
- Foster Caregivers
- Animal-Welfare Volunteers

FOA can progressively build a searchable network/database of these organizations and individuals.

### 6. Adoption Ecosystem

Because FOA intends to **facilitate** animal adoption rather than necessarily operate as an animal shelter itself, adoption should be treated as a distinct stakeholder ecosystem.

- Prospective Adopters
- Animal Shelters / NGOs offering animals for adoption
- Independent Rescuers
- Foster Caregivers
- Adoption Coordinators
- Veterinarians
- Existing Animal Guardians / Pet Owners, where relevant

### 7. Community & Public Stakeholders

These are people who may initially encounter FOA through its website, social media or activities and subsequently become part of the movement.

- Website Visitors
- Social-Media Followers
- Registered Community Members
- Volunteers
- Animal Lovers
- Parents and Families
- General Public

### 8. Donors, Funding & Support Stakeholders

These stakeholders provide the financial and institutional support necessary to scale FOA.

- Individual Donors
- Recurring Donors / Supporters
- Corporate Sponsors (they should have the option of disclosing their names)
- CSR Partners
- Foundations
- Institutional Donors

- Student / Young Creators
- Parents or Guardians of Minor Creators
- Auction Buyers
- Auction Administrators
- Payment Service Providers
- Logistics / Delivery Partners, where physical artwork is delivered

- Finance and Accounts Team
- Donors

### 11. Technology & Service Ecosystem

- FOA Engineering / Development Team

- Payment Gateway Providers

### 12. Government, Regulatory & External Stakeholders

## Competition Management Workflow

### Phase 1 — Competition Creation & Announcement

FOA Admin creates the competition on the platform.

**FOA Admin defines:**

- Competition name and theme, animal-welfare message
- Competition type — Drawing / Painting / Art / Craft / Essay / Photography / Videography
- Eligibility and age groups
- Individual or institutional participation
- Registration opening and closing dates
- Competition / submission dates
- Rules and guidelines
- Judging criteria
- Prizes and recognition
- Whether physical submission is required (top 5, top 10 etc.)
- Whether entries may be displayed publicly
- Permission for physical artwork to be subsequently auctioned
- Terms, consent and permissions

**Output:** Competition becomes live on the FOA website and can be promoted to schools, institutions and individual participants.

### Phase 2 — Institution & Participant Registration

FOA should support **two separate participation routes**.

#### Route A — Participation Through School / Institution

**Step 1: Institution Registration**

School/college registers with FOA and provides:

- Institution name
- Pin code
- Address / city / country
- Institution contact details
- Principal — email, phone
- Authorised person — email, phone
- Expected number of participants

**Step 2: Institution Competition Registration**

The institution receives:

- Institution ID
- Competition registration reference
- Coordinator dashboard/access

#### Route B — Individual Participation

A student should **not be required to participate through a school**.

The participant selects **"Participate as an Individual"** and registers directly with FOA.

Basic information collected:

- Name
- Age / date of birth
- Phone
- Pin code
- Age category
- City / State / Country
- School/college name, if applicable
- Parent/guardian information where required
- Competition selected
- Communication consent
- Required competition/participation consent

The participant receives a:

**Login — FOA Participant ID + Competition Registration Number**

This creates:

- **School Route:** School → Coordinator → Students → Competition
- **Individual Route:** Student → FOA → Competition

### Phase 3 — Competition Preparation & Conduct

After registration closes, FOA prepares participants and institutions for the competition.

FOA communicates:

- Competition date/time
- Theme/topic — confirmation
- Permitted materials
- Duration, where applicable
- Submission procedure
- Physical artwork requirements
- Important deadlines
- Contact/help information

**Competition Conduct**

Depending upon the competition, it may be:

- Conducted physically within participating schools
- Or online (essay, videography etc.)
- Completed independently by individual participants

For institution-based competitions, the designated teacher/coordinator can certify that the competition was conducted according to FOA guidelines.

### Phase 4 — Digital Submission

Every participant's work should first enter the FOA system digitally.

This phase applies only where FOA requires the original physical work.

FOA identifies entries requiring physical submission.

Participant/institution receives:

- Courier timeline

The artwork is couriered to the designated FOA facility.

The system status becomes:

**Top Digital Submission Received → Physical Artwork Awaited → Artwork Received**

### Phase 6 — School-Level Judging & Shortlisting

After conducting the competition, each participating school/institution will conduct the **first level of evaluation**.

FOA will provide a standardized judging framework to maintain consistency across participating institutions.

**School Responsibilities**

- Appoint an appropriate judging panel.
- Evaluate entries according to FOA's judging criteria.
- Declare **1st, 2nd and 3rd positions** (or top 5) at the school level for each applicable category.
- Select the prescribed number of entries (for example, **Top 5 or Top 10**) for advancement to the next level.
- Upload the shortlisted entries to the FOA portal.
- Certify that the competition and judging were conducted according to FOA guidelines.

### Phase 7 — Digital Submission of Shortlisted Entries

Only shortlisted entries will proceed to FOA-level evaluation.

The school coordinator will upload:

- High-quality photograph/scan of the artwork
- Participant ID
- Competition Entry ID
- School/institution details
- Competition category
- Title of artwork
- Short description/message, where applicable
- School-level position/ranking
- Required declarations/consents

At this stage, **physical artwork remains with the school/student**.

The system generates:

**School Shortlisted → Digital Entry Submitted to FOA**

### Phase 9 — FOA-Level Judging

FOA appoints an appropriate jury/panel to evaluate shortlisted entries digitally.

Judges evaluate entries using the standardized FOA judging framework.

Depending upon the scale of participation, judging may operate at multiple levels:

**School → City/District → State → National → Global**

FOA does not necessarily need to activate every level initially. Additional levels can be introduced as participation grows. (A Grade, B Grade **or gold / silver / bronze category**)

The platform should maintain scores, rankings and judge comments where appropriate.

**Output:**

- Finalists
- Winners
- Runners-up
- Special Mentions / Recognitions
- Entries considered suitable for FOA's fundraising auction

### Phase 10 — Results & Recognition

After final approval by FOA, results are published on the platform and social media.

Recognition can operate at different levels:

- **All Eligible Participants** — Participation Certificate
- **School-Level Winners** — School-Level Merit / Winner Certificate
- **FOA Finalists** — Finalist Certificate
- **National / Global Winners**
  - Winner / Runner-up Certificate
  - Prize / Trophy / Medal, where applicable
  - Featured recognition on FOA's platform

Certificates should contain a **unique certificate number and/or QR verification mechanism**.

Schools should be able to download certificates for their participating students from their dashboard.

Individual participants should be able to download certificates directly from their FOA account.

### Phase 11 — Digital Gallery & Social-Media Showcase

Selected competition entries will be showcased through the **FOA Digital Gallery**, subject to the required permissions.

The gallery may display:

- Artwork
- Artwork title
- Participant's approved display name
- Age/category
- School/institution
- City/country, where appropriate
- Competition
- Recognition received
- Short message/story associated with the artwork

Selected entries may also be featured through FOA's social-media channels and animal-welfare awareness campaigns.

This allows the competition content to serve FOA's larger objective:

**Student Creativity → Public Awareness → Compassion for Animals**

### Phase 14 — Physical Artwork Verification

Upon receipt, FOA verifies:

- Artwork ID
- Creator/participant
- Correspondence with the digital submission
- Physical condition
- Dimensions/material
- Required consent/documentation

FOA records the condition of the artwork upon receipt.

Status becomes: **Physical Artwork Verified**

The artwork can then proceed to exhibition and/or auction.

### Phase 15 — FOA Fundraising Auction

Approved artwork may be listed through the FOA auction platform.

Each listing may include:

- Artwork image
- Title
- Creator information, subject to consent
- Story/message behind the artwork
- Competition/award information
- Base/reserve price, where applicable
- Auction opening and closing dates
- Bid information

Workflow:

**Artwork Listed → Bidding → Winning Bid → Payment → Confirmation → Delivery**

The corresponding financial transaction should be recorded within FOA's finance system for transparency and reporting.

### Phase 16 — Post-Competition Community Engagement

The competition should become the **beginning of the participant's relationship with FOA**, rather than the end.

After completion, participants can be invited to:

- Participate in future FOA competitions
- Join the FOA community
- Volunteer
- Join/start an FOA school or college club
- Participate in animal-welfare awareness campaigns
- Attend FOA events/workshops
- Become an FOA Ambassador when eligible

The platform should maintain the participant's engagement history:

**Participant → School Winner → FOA Finalist → Community Member → Volunteer → Ambassador**

This supports FOA's long-term objective of using participation and creativity to nurture lifelong compassion for animals.

This version deliberately keeps **competition judging (Phases 6–10)**, **public engagement (11)**, **physical artwork (12–14)**, **fundraising (15)**, and **long-term engagement (16)** as separate processes. That separation will also make the software much easier for the engineering team to design.
